# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Scanarr is primarily for a self-hosting manga reader who wants one personal place to browse sources, collect series, download chapters, and read reliably over time. They are not looking for a generic admin dashboard. They are managing a living library they care about, often across long-running series, intermittent downloads, and multiple sources.

## Product Purpose
Scanarr exists to make self-hosted manga reading feel organized, dependable, and worth returning to. It should help users discover series, keep their library current, download and store chapters locally, and move into reading with minimal friction. Success looks like clear library/discovery workflows, trustworthy state visibility around downloads and chapter progress, and a reader experience that feels fast and intentional rather than cobbled together.

## Brand Personality
Sharp, energetic, and focused. The interface should feel manga-native and alive, but still trustworthy enough for daily use. It should feel more like a tuned reading tool with momentum than a cold operations console, while staying legible and calm enough that the user can stay in flow.

## Anti-references
Scanarr should not feel like a generic SaaS dashboard, a neon cyberpunk gimmick, an editorial luxury magazine, or a harsh terminal-first developer tool. It should avoid over-branding, visual irony, and any aesthetic move that makes reading or library management feel secondary to the interface itself.

## Design Principles
1. Reading first, library close behind. Discovery and library clarity should get the user to the right series fast, and the path into reading should feel direct.
2. State should be visible at a glance. Download, unread, progress, and source state must be easy to scan without turning the UI into an ops panel.
3. Energy without noise. Scanarr can feel sharp and kinetic, but motion, color, and contrast must always serve task flow and never compete with content.
4. Collection matters. Covers, series identity, and library organization should make the personal library feel owned and cared for, not just stored.
5. Familiar tools, distinctive tone. Core controls should feel trustworthy and conventional, while the overall system still carries a specific manga-tech personality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Use WCAG AA as the baseline, with reduced motion treated as a first-class requirement rather than an afterthought. Core reading, navigation, search, filtering, and status communication should remain clear under high density, low attention, and non-animated conditions. Color should never be the only carrier of important status, and contrast must hold up across dark surfaces, muted metadata, and cover-heavy layouts.
